## 要約

脳から頸部リンパ系への経路と、将来の手術研究で検討しうる標的を調べた探索的献体研究である。本文の限界節によれば検体は**健常7体とAD確認1体**で、髄膜の観察3体、頭頸部経路のマッピング3体、吻合模擬操作などを組み合わせた。認知症患者に治療を行った研究ではない。

髄膜または頭頂部へのICG注入後、頭蓋底を経てLevel II深頸リンパ節へ向かう蛍光を観察し、組織染色でリンパ組織を確認した。AD献体1体ではLevel IIリンパ節がβアミロイド陽性、Level III〜V・鼠径部は陰性だった。近傍静脈を用いるロボット支援LNVAの模擬吻合も実施できた。これは標的の位置と操作可能性についての予備情報である。

重要なのは、**硬膜でICGが静脈とリンパ管をともに染めた**点である。死後の非生理条件では受動拡散や注入アーチファクトも否定できず、生体の機能的排液路を確定したとはいえない。βアミロイド排出量、認知機能改善、治療効果、安全性、術後開存はいずれも検証しておらず、認知症治療を支持する臨床的有効性の証拠として扱わない。

> [!Abstract]
>
> BACKGROUND: Lymphatic surgery of the head and neck has been proposed as a potential approach to prevent, delay, or treat Alzheimer's disease (AD). This exploratory anatomical study undertook to map and evaluate potential targets for surgical feasibility studies.
> METHODS: This exploratory anatomical and preliminary laboratory study was supported by fresh cadaver dissection, with indocyanine green (ICG) lymphography to visualize the drainage pathway. Immunohistochemistry was used to confirm the presence of lymphatic tissues and to detect β-amyloid in lymphatic tissues from a cadaver with confirmed dementia. A simulated lymph node-to-vein anastomosis (LNVA) was performed using robotic-assisted supermicrosurgery with the Symani Surgical System (Medical Microinstruments, Inc., Jacksonville, FL, United States).
> RESULTS: After injection of ICG at the dural meninges, fluorescence progressed toward the skull base to the Level II deep cervical lymph nodes (dCLNs). When injected at the parietal lymphatics, ICG drained past the deep digastric point to the Level II cervical lymph nodes (CLNs). Veins localized adjacent to dCLNs at the deep digastric point and Level II were suggested as candidate surgical targets for LNVA. Histology of lymph nodes from a dementia-confirmed cadaver identified β-amyloid at Level II dCLNs but not at lower levels or in the groin. A robotic-assisted microsurgical LNVA was shown to be feasible.
> CONCLUSIONS: This study found evidence supporting a posterior pathway from human dural meninges to the dCLNs and identified potential surgical candidates for AD intervention. Further research is warranted to build on the feasibility of this approach.
