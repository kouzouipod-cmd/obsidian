## 要約

四肢リンパ浮腫に対するリンパ管静脈吻合（LVA）の術後開存を扱うシステマティックレビュー。2,011件をスクリーニングし、観察研究12件とRCT1件、計1,089患者を採用した。抄録では短期を12か月以下、長期を12か月超と定義している。異質性が大きいため正式なメタ解析は行わず、記述的・ナラティブに結果を整理した。

治療的LVAの報告開存率は短期32〜93%、長期36〜97%と幅がある。予防的LVAでは18か月で80%の報告があった。ICGリンパ管造影を用いた研究はリンパシンチグラフィを用いた研究より低い短期開存率を報告する傾向があり、画像が捉える対象や深達度の違いによる検出差を考える必要がある。

長期開存が可能である一方、単一の標準開存率を定める根拠にはならない。全文では10/13研究が深刻なバイアスリスクと評価され、吻合部の直接的な流れと四肢全体の間接的排液パターンが混在する。開存と持続的な臨床改善を分けて評価し、画像法・定義・患者背景・吻合単位をそろえた前向き研究が求められる。

> [!Abstract]
>
> BACKGROUND: The clinical effectiveness of lymphovenous anastomosis (LVA) for extremity lymphedema often decreases after 6-12 months. This waning effect is believed to be associated with loss of anastomotic patency, but patency rates remain poorly defined in the current literature. Consequently, the goal of this systematic review is to summarize the available evidence on short- (≤12 mo) and long-term (>12 mo) patency following supermicrosurgical LVA.
> 
> METHODS: A preferred reporting items for systematic reviews and meta-analyses-compliant systematic literature search for lymphedema, LVA, and patency/imaging was conducted in Embase, Medline (Ovid), and Web of Science. Citation searching via Lens.org, Scopus, and Web of Science supplemented the primary search. Risk of bias was assessed using ROBINS-I and RoB-2.0 tools. Study results were tabulated and summarized narratively.
> 
> RESULTS: A total of 2011 records were screened, of which twelve observational studies and one randomized controlled trial (1089 patients) met the inclusion criteria. Short-term patency after therapeutic LVA ranged from 32% to 93% and long-term patency from 36% to 97%. Prophylactic LVA demonstrated comparable durability with a patency rate of 80% after 18 months. Importantly, studies utilizing indocyanine green lymphography consistently reported lower short-term patency rates than those using lymphoscintigraphy. The majority of studies exhibited serious risk of bias.
> 
> CONCLUSIONS: Long-term patency after LVA appears achievable in a substantial proportion of patients with extremity lymphedema. However, the marked variability in reported patency rates, serious risk of bias, and heterogeneity in imaging methods and patency definitions highlights the need for standardized postoperative patency assessment.
